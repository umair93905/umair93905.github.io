As the second semester approached its final weeks, students entered one of the busiest and most demanding periods of the academic year. Months of effort spent on projects, assignments, laboratory work, and practical activities were now leading toward project evaluations and final submissions. This stage was important because it provided an opportunity to present our work, demonstrate our understanding, and receive feedback from teachers.

Among the various academic responsibilities during this period, the Database Systems project exhibition was one of the most significant experiences. It allowed students to showcase their projects and explain the knowledge and skills they had developed throughout the semester. While completing a project is an achievement in itself, presenting and defending it before teachers requires an entirely different set of skills.

## Presenting the Leukemia Prediction Project

One of the most memorable experiences of this period was presenting our Database Systems project, titled "Leukemia Blood Cancer Survival Rate Prediction." The project represented weeks of planning, research, database design, and implementation. By the time the exhibition arrived, our team had invested considerable effort into developing and refining the project.

The exhibition provided an opportunity to explain the project's objectives, database structure, and practical applications. We discussed how database concepts such as normalization, relationships, ER diagrams, and SQL queries were applied during development. Presenting the project helped me realize the importance of communicating technical ideas clearly and effectively.

Although I was initially nervous about presenting before teachers, the experience became more comfortable as the discussion progressed. Explaining the project strengthened my confidence and helped me better understand the practical significance of the work we had completed.

## Answering Questions During the Exhibition

An important part of the exhibition involved answering questions from teachers. The purpose was not only to evaluate the project itself but also to assess each student's understanding of the concepts used during development. Teachers asked questions about database design decisions, implementation techniques, and the reasoning behind various project components.

Preparing for these questions required more than simply memorizing information. It was necessary to understand every aspect of the project thoroughly. Fortunately, the guidance provided by Dr. Bilal Ahmad throughout the semester helped us develop a strong understanding of database concepts and project requirements.

During the questioning session, I realized that technical knowledge becomes much more valuable when it can be explained confidently. The experience highlighted the importance of communication skills in addition to technical abilities. Engineers and technology professionals are often required to explain their work to others, and this exhibition provided valuable practice in that area.

The interaction with teachers also allowed me to identify areas where my understanding was strong and areas where I could continue improving. As a result, the exhibition became both an evaluation activity and a meaningful learning experience.

## Video Presentation and Final Lab Submissions

After the exhibition, Dr. Bilal Ahmad assigned another interesting task. Students were instructed to prepare a video presentation related to their project. The objective was to explain the project's purpose, discuss the technologies used, and demonstrate our understanding of the work in a professional manner.

Recording the video required preparation because speaking confidently in front of a camera is very different from participating in a classroom presentation. I had to organize my thoughts carefully, explain technical concepts clearly, and present information in a structured way. The task also required the inclusion of supporting visuals, which helped improve the overall quality of the presentation.

At the same time, students were also completing laboratory manual submissions for multiple courses. These manuals represented months of practical work and required careful documentation. Organizing screenshots, formatting reports, and ensuring that every task was properly included demanded significant attention and effort.

Managing project exhibitions, video presentations, and laboratory submissions simultaneously created considerable academic pressure. However, this experience taught me valuable lessons about professionalism, communication, and responsibility. Looking back, this period was one of the most educational phases of my second semester because it combined technical knowledge, presentation skills, and practical documentation into a single learning experience.
